WELCOME

Congratulations on taking the first step toward turning your love of baking into a wedding cake business. This ebook was written for home bakers who want to offer beautiful, delicious wedding cakes without needing a culinary degree or a commercial kitchen.

Inside, you'll find eight tried-and-tested cake flavors that are popular with couples, reliable enough to bake ahead of a wedding day, and simple enough to make confidently in a home kitchen. Every recipe includes ingredient lists, clear step-by-step instructions, and a business tip to help you price, market, or serve that flavor to real clients.

Before you start baking, read the next two short chapters — they cover the essential equipment you'll need and the habits that separate a hobby baker from a dependable wedding cake business.

GETTING STARTED: EQUIPMENT & KITCHEN SETUP

You don't need a professional bakery to make wedding cakes — but a few key tools will make your results far more consistent.

Essential Equipment

A stand mixer (a hand mixer works for smaller cakes, but a stand mixer saves time on larger orders)

Round cake pans in at least three sizes (6", 8", and 10") for tiered cakes

A digital kitchen scale — weighing ingredients gives far more consistent results than measuring cups

An offset spatula and a bench scraper for smooth buttercream finishes

A turntable for icing and decorating

Cake boards, dowels or straws, and a sturdy cake box for transport

An oven thermometer — most home ovens run hotter or cooler than their dial suggests

Kitchen Habits Worth Building Now

Always weigh your ingredients when possible; wedding cakes need to be consistent from tier to tier

Bake a full test cake — flavor, crumb, and stacking — before offering a flavor to clients

Keep a written recipe card for every flavor with your exact quantities, oven time, and notes

Check your local cottage food laws before selling cakes made in a home kitchen — rules vary by region

PRICING & SELLING YOUR CAKES

Many new bakers under-price their cakes because they only account for ingredients. Your price needs to cover far more than flour and sugar.

What to Include in Your Price

Ingredients (cake, filling, and buttercream or fondant)

Your time — mixing, baking, filling, crumb-coating, decorating, and delivery

Equipment wear and utility costs (oven, mixer, electricity)

Delivery and setup at the venue, including any travel time

A buffer for a tasting sample, if you offer one

A Simple Pricing Starting Point

Many home bakers price wedding cakes per serving, starting around $4–$8 per slice for a simple buttercream cake and increasing for elaborate decoration, sugar flowers, or premium flavors such as champagne or almond-raspberry. Always adjust for your local market and ingredient costs.

Offer a small tasting box with two or three flavors from this collection — vanilla, chocolate, and one specialty flavor are a strong starting trio that shows range without overwhelming a new client.

A NOTE ON TIERED CAKES

All eight recipes below are written for a single 8-inch round layer cake (two layers), which is the easiest way to test a flavor. To build a tiered wedding cake, simply multiply the recipe for each tier size and always insert dowels or straws into the lower tiers before stacking, so the weight of the upper tiers doesn't compress the cake below.

Every cake in this collection can be baked up to two days ahead, wrapped tightly in plastic while still slightly warm, and refrigerated or frozen — this actually improves the texture and makes assembly week far less stressful.

THE RECIPES

Each recipe below makes one 8-inch, two-layer cake (about 16 servings). Scale up for larger tiers as needed.

1. Classic Vanilla Bean Wedding Cake

The most requested wedding flavor — light, moist, and a perfect base for any filling.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 20 minutes

Bake Time: 30–35 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Cake

3 cups (375g) cake flour

1 tablespoon baking powder

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 cup (225g) unsalted butter, softened

2 cups (400g) granulated sugar

4 large eggs, room temperature

1 tablespoon vanilla bean paste (or pure vanilla extract)

1 cup (240ml) whole milk, room temperature

Vanilla Buttercream

1 1/2 cups (340g) unsalted butter, softened

5 cups (600g) powdered sugar

2 tablespoons heavy cream

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Pinch of salt

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk the cake flour, baking powder, and salt together in a bowl and set aside.

Beat the butter and sugar together for 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition, then mix in the vanilla bean paste.

Add the flour mixture in three additions, alternating with the milk, mixing just until combined.

Divide the batter evenly between the pans and bake for 30–35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack and cool completely before filling.

For the buttercream, beat the butter until smooth, then gradually add powdered sugar, cream, vanilla, and salt, beating until light and spreadable.

Fill and stack the layers, then crumb-coat and finish with a smooth or textured buttercream finish.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Vanilla is the safest 'default' flavor for tastings — pair it with a fun filling (raspberry, salted caramel, or lemon curd) so even a plain-sounding flavor feels custom to the couple.

2. Rich Chocolate Fudge Cake

A deeply chocolatey layer cake that stays moist for days — ideal for make-ahead assembly.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 20 minutes

Bake Time: 32–35 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Cake

2 cups (250g) all-purpose flour

3/4 cup (75g) unsweetened cocoa powder

2 teaspoons baking soda

1/2 teaspoon baking powder

1 teaspoon salt

2 cups (400g) granulated sugar

2 large eggs

1 cup (240ml) buttermilk

1 cup (240ml) hot coffee (or hot water)

1/2 cup (120ml) vegetable oil

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Chocolate Ganache Filling

8 oz (225g) semi-sweet chocolate, chopped

1 cup (240ml) heavy cream

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, baking powder, and salt.

In a separate bowl, whisk the sugar, eggs, buttermilk, oil, and vanilla until smooth.

Combine the wet and dry ingredients, then carefully stir in the hot coffee — the batter will be thin; this is correct.

Divide between the pans and bake for 32–35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out with just a few moist crumbs.

Cool completely before filling and frosting.

For the ganache, heat the cream until steaming, pour over the chopped chocolate, and let sit 2 minutes before stirring until smooth. Cool until spreadable before using as a filling.

Frost the outside with vanilla or chocolate buttercream (see Chapter 5 base recipe).

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Chocolate is the flavor most couples choose as one of two tiers on a mixed-flavor cake — always keep it in your standard flavor menu, since it rarely gets skipped in a tasting.

3. Southern Red Velvet Cake

A striking crimson crumb with a hint of cocoa, finished in classic cream cheese frosting.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Bake Time: 28–30 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Cake

2 1/2 cups (315g) all-purpose flour

1 1/2 cups (300g) granulated sugar

1 teaspoon baking soda

1 tablespoon cocoa powder

1 teaspoon salt

1 1/2 cups (355ml) vegetable oil

1 cup (240ml) buttermilk, room temperature

2 large eggs

2 tablespoons red food coloring gel

1 teaspoon white vinegar

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Cream Cheese Frosting

16 oz (450g) cream cheese, softened

1/2 cup (115g) unsalted butter, softened

4 cups (480g) powdered sugar

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the flour, sugar, baking soda, cocoa powder, and salt.

In another bowl, whisk the oil, buttermilk, eggs, food coloring, vinegar, and vanilla.

Combine wet and dry ingredients and mix just until smooth — do not overmix.

Divide between the pans and bake for 28–30 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely before frosting.

Beat the cream cheese and butter together until smooth, then add powdered sugar and vanilla, beating until light and fluffy.

Fill and frost — red velvet is traditionally finished with a thin, semi-naked coat to let the red crumb peek through.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Cream cheese frosting is softer than buttercream and doesn't hold up as well in heat — for outdoor or summer weddings, stabilize it with an extra cup of powdered sugar or offer this flavor for indoor, climate-controlled venues only.

4. Lemon Elderflower Cake

A bright, floral cake that has become a favorite for spring and summer weddings.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Bake Time: 28–32 minutes

Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Cake

3 cups (375g) cake flour

1 tablespoon baking powder

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 cup (225g) unsalted butter, softened

1 3/4 cups (350g) granulated sugar

4 large eggs

Zest of 2 lemons

1/4 cup (60ml) fresh lemon juice

2 tablespoons elderflower cordial (or 1 tsp elderflower extract)

3/4 cup (180ml) whole milk

Elderflower Buttercream

1 1/2 cups (340g) unsalted butter, softened

5 cups (600g) powdered sugar

2 tablespoons elderflower cordial

1 tablespoon lemon juice

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the cake flour, baking powder, and salt.

Beat the butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes.

Add eggs one at a time, then mix in the lemon zest, lemon juice, and elderflower cordial.

Alternate adding the flour mixture and milk, mixing just until combined.

Divide between pans and bake for 28–32 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely. For the buttercream, beat butter until smooth, then add powdered sugar, cordial, and lemon juice, beating until fluffy.

Fill with lemon curd for extra brightness, then frost with the elderflower buttercream.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Floral and citrus flavors photograph beautifully with pressed flowers or candied lemon slices — this is an easy upsell for couples planning a garden or outdoor wedding.

5. Carrot Cake with Cream Cheese Frosting

A warmly spiced, naturally moist cake that appeals to guests who prefer a less-sweet option.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Bake Time: 35–40 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Cake

2 1/2 cups (315g) all-purpose flour

2 teaspoons baking powder

1 teaspoon baking soda

1 tablespoon ground cinnamon

1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 1/2 cups (355ml) vegetable oil

1 1/2 cups (300g) light brown sugar

4 large eggs

3 cups (300g) finely grated carrots

1 cup (100g) chopped walnuts or pecans (optional)

Cream Cheese Frosting

16 oz (450g) cream cheese, softened

1/2 cup (115g) unsalted butter, softened

4 cups (480g) powdered sugar

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt.

In another bowl, whisk the oil, brown sugar, and eggs until well combined.

Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture, then fold in the grated carrots and nuts.

Divide between pans and bake for 35–40 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely before frosting.

Beat the cream cheese and butter until smooth, then add powdered sugar and vanilla, beating until fluffy.

Fill and frost, leaving the sides semi-naked or fully coated with a rustic finish.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

List this as your 'alternative' flavor on your menu — many couples keep one non-chocolate, non-vanilla tier specifically for guests with different tastes, and carrot cake fills that role reliably.

6. Champagne Cake

A delicate, celebratory cake made with real champagne — an elegant, on-theme choice for the occasion.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Bake Time: 28–30 minutes

Difficulty: Moderate

Cake

3 cups (375g) cake flour

1 tablespoon baking powder

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 cup (225g) unsalted butter, softened

1 3/4 cups (350g) granulated sugar

4 large egg whites, room temperature

1 cup (240ml) champagne or sparkling wine, at room temperature

1/4 cup (60ml) whole milk

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Champagne Buttercream

1 1/2 cups (340g) unsalted butter, softened

5 cups (600g) powdered sugar

3 tablespoons champagne, reduced by half and cooled

Pinch of salt

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the cake flour, baking powder, and salt.

Beat the butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes.

Add the egg whites in two additions, beating well after each.

Combine the champagne, milk, and vanilla in a small bowl.

Alternate adding the flour mixture and champagne mixture, mixing just until combined.

Divide between pans and bake for 28–30 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely. For the buttercream, simmer 3 tablespoons of champagne until reduced by half, then cool fully before beating into the butter and powdered sugar.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Champagne cake is a strong premium up-charge item — many bakers price it 15–20% above their standard vanilla tier because of the specialty ingredient and its 'special occasion' appeal.

7. Funfetti Confetti Cake

A playful, colorful crowd-pleaser that works beautifully for rehearsal dinners and casual weddings.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 20 minutes

Bake Time: 28–30 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Cake

3 cups (375g) cake flour

1 tablespoon baking powder

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 cup (225g) unsalted butter, softened

2 cups (400g) granulated sugar

4 large eggs

1 tablespoon vanilla extract

1 cup (240ml) whole milk

3/4 cup (140g) rainbow sprinkles (jimmies, not nonpareils)

Vanilla Buttercream

1 1/2 cups (340g) unsalted butter, softened

5 cups (600g) powdered sugar

2 tablespoons heavy cream

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the cake flour, baking powder, and salt.

Beat the butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes.

Add the eggs one at a time, then mix in the vanilla.

Alternate adding the flour mixture and milk, mixing just until combined.

Gently fold in the sprinkles by hand — do not overmix, or the colors will bleed into the batter.

Divide between pans and bake for 28–30 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely, then fill and frost with vanilla buttercream.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Use jimmie-style sprinkles rather than nonpareils — nonpareils bleed color into the batter and turn it grey. This small detail is a common beginner mistake worth avoiding in front of paying clients.

8. Almond Raspberry Cake

A refined, nutty cake with bright raspberry filling — a popular choice for a more grown-up flavor profile.

Servings: 16 servings (one 8-inch, 2-layer cake)

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Bake Time: 30–32 minutes

Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Cake

2 3/4 cups (345g) cake flour

1 tablespoon baking powder

1/2 teaspoon salt

1 cup (225g) unsalted butter, softened

1 3/4 cups (350g) granulated sugar

4 large eggs

1 teaspoon almond extract

1 teaspoon vanilla extract

1 cup (240ml) whole milk

Raspberry Filling & Buttercream

1 cup (320g) seedless raspberry jam

1 1/2 cups (340g) unsalted butter, softened

5 cups (600g) powdered sugar

2 tablespoons heavy cream

1/2 teaspoon almond extract

Method

Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round cake pans.

Whisk together the cake flour, baking powder, and salt.

Beat the butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes.

Add the eggs one at a time, then mix in the almond and vanilla extracts.

Alternate adding the flour mixture and milk, mixing just until combined.

Divide between pans and bake for 30–32 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Cool completely. Spread a thin layer of raspberry jam directly onto one cake layer before adding buttercream, so the fruit flavor comes through clearly.

For the buttercream, beat butter until smooth, then add powdered sugar, cream, and almond extract, beating until fluffy. Fill and frost.

💰 BUSINESS TIP  

Always ask about nut allergies before offering almond-based flavors — keep a nut-free alternative (such as vanilla or lemon) ready to suggest for any guest list with allergy concerns.

FINAL NOTES

Every recipe in this collection has been kept intentionally simple: no fondant sculpting, no complex sugar work, and no ingredients that are hard to find. The goal is dependable flavor and texture — the two things that make couples recommend a home baker to their friends.

As your business grows, you can layer on more advanced skills — sugar flowers, hand-painted tiers, or elaborate piping — but the recipes here will remain a solid foundation underneath any level of decoration.

Wishing you every success with your home wedding cake business.
